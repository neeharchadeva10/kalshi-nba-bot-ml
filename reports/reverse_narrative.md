# Reverse test: Kalshi as the fair price, trading on Polymarket US

*Backtest only: no orders were placed. Data comes from Polymarket US's public market data (no account) and
Kalshi's public data. The tables below are regenerated by `kpm reverse`; this summary was written after
reading them.*

## Bottom line

**No edge in trading Polymarket US against Kalshi's price.** The one thing that survives is a rare two-leg
arbitrage, and it is too small to build around. It turned up 13 times, in 7 of 158 games, and made $14 in
total on 100-contract trades.

- **Polymarket US also follows Kalshi.** This rests on 53 gaps in 17 games, so it's a small sample. When
  Polymarket US was cheap compared with Kalshi, it was Polymarket US that moved: by kickoff it had moved
  toward Kalshi in 43–67% of these gaps, and Kalshi toward it in 0–17%. Its average move was 13–18¢, against
  Kalshi's 0.3–0.5¢.
- **Cheap moments were rare.** Polymarket US's ask sat more than 2¢ below Kalshi's fair price, after its fee,
  in about 0.03% of minutes.
- **Buying on Polymarket US only looked profitable because of bad quotes.** On all trades the later weeks
  showed +5.1¢ per contract for "sell when it catches up" and +12.9¢ for "hold to settlement". Both
  confidence intervals include zero, and the gains came from a handful of trades on prices nobody could
  trade:
  - NYJ–BAL: one minute in which Polymarket US's history swapped the two teams' prices (+73¢ on paper).
  - TEN–JAX, NYJ–BUF, NO–ATL: placeholder 50¢ asks in very wide books.
- **With only clean prices, the edge disappears.** Keeping just the entries where both venues' books were
  tight (3¢ or less):
  - "Sell when it catches up" loses 2.2¢ per contract (11 trades, CI −4.2 to −0.4).
  - "Hold" gives +4.5¢ (11 trades, CI −15 to +25), which is game-outcome noise.
- **The two-leg arbitrage was real but tiny.** The trade buys the cheap side on Polymarket US and the
  opposite side on Kalshi, so it pays $1 whoever wins.
  - All 13 trades were profitable as locked in: 2.6¢ per contract on average in weeks 9–13 (4 trades) and
    0.4¢ in later weeks (9 trades). No settlement mismatches.
  - Kalshi's side was confirmed by real trade prints. Polymarket US publishes no volume, so we can't confirm
    100 contracts were available on that side.

## How it was tested

- **Coverage:**
  - Polymarket US listed NFL game markets from week 9 (Nov 2, 2025): 161 games, of which 158 are analyzed.
  - 3 games are waiting in `review/manual_review.csv`.
  - Thresholds were chosen on weeks 9–13 (73 games) and tested on weeks 14–18 plus the playoffs (85 games).
    No setting reached 50 trades in weeks 9–13, so every result is flagged "small sample".
- **Prices:** Polymarket US history gives a 1-minute ask and bid for each team, with typical spreads of
  1.2–2¢. Minutes with no two-sided book (e.g. a 0.50/0.50 placeholder) or a spread wider than 10¢ were
  excluded.
- **Kalshi fair price:** the average of what both Kalshi team markets imply.
- **Fees:**
  - Polymarket US taker fee 0.0695 × p × (1−p), rounded to the cent. This is the schedule effective
    Oct 1, 2026, applied to 2025 data as "what you'd pay today".
  - Kalshi taker fee as in the main report.
- **Matching:** same process as the main study. Rules plus an AI review of all 322 pairs found the same game
  and outcome. Differences: cancellation pays a "fair price" on Kalshi vs 50-50 on Polymarket US, postponement
  windows differ, and Polymarket US's text is silent on ties.

## Limits

- **Small samples:** 4–22 trades per result.
- **No Polymarket US volume or depth:** we can't check that its quotes could have filled, and a 1-minute
  snapshot can't show sub-minute dislocations.
- **"Both books tight" is post-hoc:** it was defined after seeing the trades and isn't part of the
  early/later protocol. It is a standard data-quality filter, though, and it removes exactly the trades that
  were inspected and found to be glitches.
- **No 2025 history before week 9:** Polymarket US had no NFL markets then.
